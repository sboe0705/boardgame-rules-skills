# Overview, Global Parameters, Game Board, Tiles, Markers, Player Boards, Cards, Components

Page references: **EN** = English rulebook (16 pages; printed page numbers equal PDF page numbers, the cover is p. 1).

---

## Game overview
**Rulebook:** EN p. 3

- Each player controls a **corporation** and buys and plays **project cards**. Projects contribute directly or indirectly to terraforming, or are business enterprises.
- To win you need a good **terraform rating (TR)** and many **victory points (VPs)**.
- **TR** goes up each time you raise a **global parameter** (temperature, oxygen or ocean). TR sets your basic income and your basic score.
- As terraforming proceeds, more projects become feasible. Extra VPs come from anything that strengthens mankind's grip on the solar system (cities, infrastructure, protecting the environment …).
- Time is measured in **generations**. Each generation: Turn Order phase → Research phase (players access new cards) → Action phase (players take turns doing 1 or 2 actions until everyone has passed) → Production phase (everyone produces resources according to the production tracks on the player board and gains income from TR). The rulebook calls the first phase "Turn Order phase" on p. 3 and "Player Order phase" on p. 7–8.
- The central board has tracks for temperature, oxygen level, TR and generations, a surface map for ocean, greenery and city tiles, a list of **standard projects** open to all players, and **milestones** and **awards** to compete for.
- **Game end:** when oxygen is at **14 %**, there are **9** oceans and temperature is at **+8 °C**.
- **Winner:** the player with the most VPs at the end. VPs come from TR, your tiles on the board, won awards, claimed milestones and VPs on cards you have played.

## Global parameters
**Rulebook:** EN p. 3

- **Temperature, oxygen and ocean** are the global parameters.
- Whenever you raise one of them, your **TR rises by the same number of steps** (higher income and score). The board picture repeats this: "Don't forget! Increasing a global parameter also increases your TR!" (p. 4).
- When a parameter has **reached its goal** it can't be raised further and gives **no TR**. You may still play cards and use actions that would raise it; just ignore that part of the effect.
- When **all three** have reached their goal, the game ends **after that generation** (after the production phase). See `game-end.md`.

Sidebar "A Terraformed Mars" (p. 3, background only): each area/tile stands for 1 % of the Martian surface, so 9 ocean tiles = 9 % ocean coverage. Air pressure (nitrogen) and Mars' weak magnetic field are **not** global parameters, but cards about them usually give a higher TR, as stated on those cards.

## Game board
**Rulebook:** EN p. 4

The numbers are the callouts in the board picture.

| # | Element | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Terraform rating (TR) track** (runs around the board edge, 1–100) | All players start at **20**. TR is your basic income (see Player boards and `generations.md`) and VPs. It rises every time you terraform. |
| 2 | **Generation track** | The generation marker measures time (rounds). It starts at **1** and moves up **on the TR track**. |
| 3 | **Solo marks** | Solo games start with TR **14** and end after generation **14** (see `variants.md`). |
| 4 | **Oxygen** (arc above the map, 0–14 %) | Global parameter, starts at **0 %**. The percentage compares to Earth's 21 %. |
| 5 | **Ocean tiles** (stack space top right) | Global parameter. Starts with **9** tiles stacked here, to be placed on the map during the game. |
| 6 | **Temperature** (thermometer on the right) | Global parameter (mean temperature at the equator), starts at **−30 °C**. It rises 2 °C per step up to +8 °C (p. 14). |
| 7 | **Bonus steps** | If you raise the parameter to this point, you also get the attached bonus. |
| 8 | **Standard projects** (list on the left) | May be used by any player regardless of cards. See `actions.md`. |
| 9 | **Milestones / Awards** (bottom) | A good source of extra VPs. See `actions.md`. |
| 10 | **Placement bonuses** | Placing a tile on an area with a printed bonus gives you the printed resources or cards. |
| 11 | **Ocean-reserved areas** (blue) | Only ocean tiles may be placed here, and ocean tiles may be placed nowhere else. There are **12** such areas (p. 15) for 9 ocean tiles. |
| 12 | **Special reserved areas** | **3** areas are reserved for specific cities. No other tiles may be placed there. |

**Bonus steps (sidebar "The Bonus Steps" and board picture, p. 4):**
- **Oxygen 8 %:** a temperature bonus (the thickening atmosphere raises the temperature). The picture shows a temperature icon at 8 %.
- **Temperature:** two **heat production** bonuses on the way up (thawing carbon dioxide). In the board picture they sit at **−24 °C** and **−20 °C** (read from the picture; the text doesn't name the values).
- **Temperature 0 °C:** water bonus (ice in the soil melts). The picture shows an **ocean tile** icon at 0 °C.
- *Interpretation:* a bonus that raises another global parameter also gives TR for that step, following the general rule above. The rulebook doesn't spell this out for bonus steps.

**Special reserved areas in the picture (p. 4):** one on the map itself labelled **Noctis City**, and two outside the planet disc (the labels read Phobos Space Haven and Ganymede Colony; they are small in the picture, so treat the names as read from the image). The matching cards say how they are used.

**Sidebar "The Map" (p. 4, background):** the map shows the Tharsis region, including Valles Marineris and 3 of the 4 great volcanoes. Ocean areas are low-lying. **Plant** bonuses lie around the equator, **steel and titanium** bonuses on mountain ridges, and **card draw** bonuses on other interesting sites (e.g. the Viking landing site).

## Tiles
**Rulebook:** EN p. 5 (symbol summary p. 15)

General:
- Before placing, check **placement restrictions**: reserved areas (ocean, specific cities), plus restrictions on the card or in the list below.
- When you place a tile you receive the **placement bonus** printed on that area, if any.
- You also get **2 M€ for each ocean tile adjacent** to the tile you place.

| Tile | Placement | Effect | Owner / VPs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ocean** (blue tile, water drop) | Only on ocean-reserved areas | Your TR +1 step | Not owned by anyone. Each ocean on the board gives a **2 M€ placement bonus** to any player who later places a tile next to it, even another ocean. |
| **Greenery** (green tile, tree) | **Must be next to another tile you own, if possible.** If you have no free area next to your tiles, or no tile at all, any available area. | Raises oxygen 1 step, if possible, and your TR. **If oxygen can't be raised, you get no TR either.** | Put your player marker on it. Worth **1 VP** at game end and gives **1 VP to every adjacent city**. |
| **City** (grey tile, skyline) | **Not next to another city.** Exception: **Noctis City** must always be placed on its reserved area. | – | Put your player marker on it. Worth **1 VP per adjacent greenery tile, regardless of owner**, at game end. |
| **Special tiles** (brown border) | As described on the card | As described on the card | Place the tile and put your player marker on it. |

- **Example (p. 5):** placing a city next to 2 different ocean tiles gives 4 M€ as placement bonus.
- **Capital (note, p. 5):** the card Capital places the unique Capital city tile. It counts and scores like a normal city tile, and additionally gives VPs for adjacent ocean tiles, as stated on the card.

## Markers
**Rulebook:** EN p. 5

- **Resource cubes:** resources are collected on the player board or on specific cards. All resource types use the same cubes: **bronze = 1, silver = 5, gold = 10**. Where a cube lies determines what it is (p. 14).
- **Player markers** (transparent cubes in your colour): mark your TR, ownership of tiles, production levels, and used blue card actions.
- **Temperature, oxygen and generation markers** (big white cubes): start at the beginning of their tracks.
- **First player marker:** shifts clockwise each generation.

## Player boards
**Rulebook:** EN p. 6

- The board tracks your **resources** (cubes in the six boxes) and your **production** (player markers on the six production tracks).
- **Icon convention:** a plain resource icon means resource cubes. A resource icon **inside a brown box** means **production** of that resource.

| # | Area | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Production tracks** | When you gain production, mark the new level with your player marker. **Production is not limited to 10.** In the production phase you add cubes equal to your production. |
| 2 | **MegaCredits (M€)** | Pay for cards and other things. **M€ income = M€ production + TR.** M€ production is the only production that can be negative, and it may never go below **−5**. |
| 3 | **Steel** | Only used to pay for cards with a **building tag**; worth **2 M€ per cube**. You may mix M€ and steel, but there is **no refund for overpaying** with steel. |
| 4 | **Titanium** | Only used to pay for cards with a **space tag**; worth **3 M€ per cube**, similar to steel. |
| 5 | **Plants** | May be converted into greenery tiles with the printed action (8 plants → greenery tile). |
| 6 | **Energy** | Used by many cards. **All leftover energy becomes heat at the beginning of the production phase.** |
| 7 | **Heat** | May be spent to raise temperature 1 step with the printed action (8 heat → temperature). |

- **Example (p. 6):** a card raises your heat production 3 steps, from 19 to 22. You show this with 2 player markers on "10" and 1 marker on "2" of the heat production track.
- Board picture details: the M€ track runs from −5 to 10, the other tracks from 0 to 10. The steel box shows "building tag : steel = 2", the titanium box "space tag : titanium = 3".
- p. 14 adds: if production exceeds 10, leave the marker on 10 and start a new marker. Because TR is added to M€ production, your total income will not be negative.

## Cards
**Rulebook:** EN p. 6

- Each player starts with a **corporation card**. During the game players buy and play **project cards**.
- Project cards come in three kinds: **active** (blue frame), **automated** (green), **event** (red). See `actions.md` for how each is placed.

Card anatomy (callouts on the example cards Tharsis Republic and Electro Catapult):

| # | Part | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tag** (top right) | Puts the card in a category that can affect or be affected by other cards or the player board (e.g. paying with steel for a building tag). |
| 2 | **Starting conditions** (corporations) | How much M€ you start with, plus other starting resources and production. Some corporations also have a fixed **first action**. |
| 3 | **Effect / action** (box with a blue ribbon) | An ongoing effect or an action. **Actions may be used only once per generation; effects are always active.** |
| 4 | **Flavor text** | Background only. |
| 5 | **Cost** (top left) | What you pay to play the card from your hand. Cards get into your hand by buying them in the research phase. |
| 6 | **Requirement** (next to the cost) | **Must be met** to play the card. Some need a global parameter to have reached a level, others can only be played while it is still low; some need certain tags or production. It only has to be met **when you play the card**, not when you use it later. You must also be able to perform the card's effects (see `actions.md`). |
| 7 | **Immediate effects** (lower panel) | Most affect your (or an opponent's) resources or production, place tiles, or do other things. |
| 8 | **VPs** (bottom right) | Some cards give VPs at game end. |

**If you are unsure how a card works, read the text in parenthesis on the card.**

Example cards shown (p. 6):
- **Tharsis Republic** (corporation, building tag): start with 40 M€; first action: place a city tile. Effect: when any city tile is placed **on Mars**, increase your M€ production 1 step; when you place a city tile, gain 3 M€.
- **Electro Catapult** (blue card, building tag, cost 17, requirement: oxygen max 8 %): action: spend 1 plant or 1 steel to gain 7 M€. Immediate effect: decrease your energy production 1 step. 1 VP.

- The setup text speaks of **10 standard corporation cards** and **2 Corporate Era corporations** plus **Beginner Corporation** cards (p. 7). *Derived:* 17 − 10 − 2 leaves 5 Beginner Corporations; the rulebook doesn't state that number.
