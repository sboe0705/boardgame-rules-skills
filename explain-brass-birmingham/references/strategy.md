# Strategy: Community Advice on How to Play Well

> **Use only on explicit request.** Read and quote this file **only** when the person explicitly asks for strategy, tactics, winning tips, opening moves or "how do I play better / win". Do **not** bring up anything from this file in a rules answer, a setup walkthrough, a quick reference or a full teach, unless strategy advice is asked for. (The rulebook's own short "Beginner Tips" are in `tips-variants.md` and stay part of the teach.)

**This is not rules content.** Everything here is **unofficial community advice** from strategy articles and player guides. It is opinion, it depends on player count, cards and opponents, and the sources sometimes disagree. Present it as advice ("experienced players recommend…"), never as a rule.

- **Source tags** in square brackets show where a point comes from (list at the end): `[ET-I]`, `[ET-M]`, `[ET-B]`, `[Steam]`, `[TTP]`, `[SE]`, `[HL]`.
- **Rule facts** carry the rulebook page (EN = DE page numbers) and are explained in the other reference files.
- **Tile values** (VPs, income, link icons, beer needs of single tile levels) and **board geography** (which towns have which spaces or lines) are quoted **from the sources**; the rules references of this skill don't list them. Tell players to check the values on their Player Mat and board.
- The sources call Iron Works also "steel mills" and Manufacturers "boxes" or "crates".

## 1. Core principles

- **Time is the limit, not money.** The game is about action efficiency: 8 / 9 / 10 rounds per era with 4 / 3 / 2 players, 2 actions per turn, 1 in the very first round (p. 6), so **31 / 35 / 39 actions** per player in the whole game. `[Steam]` `[ET-I]`
- **Target scores** against skilled players: about **200 / 175 / 155 VPs** with 2 / 3 / 4 players, i.e. about **5 VPs per action** on average. Develop and Loan score nothing, so the scoring actions must bring clearly more. `[SE]`
- **Think in VPs per action, not in "engines".** Every action should soon flip a valuable tile, enable a double rail Link, or take beer / a Merchant / a key spot away from opponents at the right moment. Money and income are tools, not goals. `[TTP]`
- **If you lose with a lot of money,** you probably built too many Coal Mines; a good game is tight: no wasted income, but also not too many loans. `[ET-I]`
- **Flip your tiles.** Only flipped tiles score (p. 7). Plan the route to a matching Merchant and the beer **before** you build a Cotton Mill, Manufacturer or Pottery; a stranded tile is lost money and actions. `[TTP]`
- **Stay focused.** Points come from building an industry out in depth; a bit of everything scores little. Mixing two selling industries (cotton + manufactured goods, or manufactured goods + pottery) means you reach the top levels of neither; the exception is the level 1 Pottery in the Rail Era. Check your starting hand for locations that fit a plan. `[Steam]` `[SE]`
- **Two kinds of industries:** Iron Works and Breweries are "universal" (everyone needs them to have a chance); Cotton Mills, Manufacturers and Potteries are the "main" industries, of which you pick one by player count, cards and Merchants in play. `[SE]`
- **Every action opens moves for the opponents** (resources, connections, spots). Before you place coal, iron or beer, ask who will use it. `[ET-B]` `[TTP]`
- **Final income is worth nothing** except as a tiebreaker (p. 7, 12): in the last rounds only VPs count.

## 2. Loans and money

- **Don't fear loans** (also the rulebook's advice, p. 12). An early loan is seen as cheaper than a late one, and early income from Coal Mines or Iron Works can make up for it. `[HL]` `[ET-I]`
- **Do the non-scoring actions in the Canal Era:** most points come in the Rail Era, so take the bulk of your Loans and Develop actions before it. `[SE]`
- **Avoid negative income** (you would pay each round, p. 6); a level 1 Coal Mine or Iron Works can lift you out of it. `[SE]`
- **A loan costs an action,** so take it with a purpose: on a turn or round in which you also flip something or place a key Link. Borrowing to build something that won't flip soon leads to "great income, no points". `[TTP]`
- **Money instead of loans:** a Coal Mine or Iron Works that sells its cubes to an empty Market (p. 9) brings cash at once; in the Rail Era a Coal Mine can replace a loan this way. `[ET-I]`
- **Target for the era change:** enough cash to take the double rail Link action (£15, p. 11) right away, ideally twice within the first two rounds, plus one strong tile that flips. `[TTP]`

## 3. Turn order

- The player who spends the **least** goes first next round; ties keep their relative order (p. 6).
- **"Free" actions set up turn order:** Scout, Loan and Develop cost no money (Develop only if iron must be bought from the Market, p. 8). `[Steam]`
- **Four actions in a row:** going last in one round and first in the next lets you set up and complete a play (build + sell, coal + rail Links) or block someone before they can react. `[Steam]`
- **The last–first cycle:** when you are last, take a Loan or spend little; when you are then first, make your big spending moves; repeat. `[SE]`
- **Example opening:** a Loan or Scout as your first action, to be early in round 2 and put an Iron Works into a town where another player has just built a Coal Mine. `[Steam]` More opening advice: section 6.
- **Know which kind of round it is:** sometimes you spend little to move first (a denial play, the best rail lines); sometimes you must spend a lot now to secure a cornerstone such as a hub Link or a critical Brewery space. `[TTP]`
- If your cards force a weak turn anyway, make it a cheap one (Develop, Loan, discard) and take the earlier turn next round. `[TTP]`

## 4. Beer: the key resource

- Beer is rated the **most important resource**: it is needed for most sales and for the double rail Link (p. 10, 11), and it is scarce and contested by everyone. `[ET-B]`
- **Take opponents' beer when you can.** You help them flip a Brewery, but if you don't take it, someone else will, and few games have plenty of beer. `[ET-M]` `[Steam]`
- **Use your own beer quickly.** Flipping your Brewery is good, but beer is too valuable to give away; sell with your own beer first and leave the board thirsty. `[ET-I]` `[TTP]`
- **Your own beer needs no connection** (p. 8), so far-away spots (the sources name Uttoxeter and Burton-on-Trent) can secure a supply that opponents can hardly reach. `[ET-I]`
- **Merchant beer: the sources disagree.** One view: it is free and brings a bonus (p. 10), so don't waste it, and early in the game it can be better than placing a Brewery of your own. `[ET-M]` `[ET-B]` The other view: reaching for Merchant beer is a typical misconception; make and use your own beer, and take Merchant beer only when yours has been taken and it is the only way to sell. `[SE]`
- **Don't worry much when others drink your beer:** the Brewery flips and you get its points. `[SE]` (Compare "use your own beer quickly" above: the sources weigh this differently.)
- **Keep brewery cards for Breweries:** never use a card that lets you build a Brewery for anything else. `[SE]`
- **Sell several tiles in one Sell action** (p. 10) when the beer allows it. This is easier in the Canal Era, because opponents can rarely reach your beer then. `[SE]`
- **Saving Canal Era beer for the Rail Era** is mostly a misconception: worth it at most if you will go first and take the double rail Link with it; used in the Canal Era, the Brewery would already have scored. `[SE]`
- **Think on three levels:** beer now (can I sell this turn?), beer later (where will it be when the big tile is ready?), and denying beer (can I empty a Brewery or a Merchant barrel so a rival's sale fails?). Taking the beer an opponent needs for a late sale often decides games. `[TTP]` `[Steam]`
- **"Beer fork":** prepare two possible sales with different beer sources and take whichever is still open on your turn. `[TTP]`
- **Supply shapes the game:** much beer on the board favours players laying many rail Links; little beer favours players building industries. `[Steam]`

## 5. The industries

### Breweries
- **The most important industry:** efficient in income and VPs, cheap, they need iron instead of coal, need no Sell action to flip, and count double for Links according to the sources. All players should build them and contest them heavily. `[ET-I]` `[ET-M]`
- **Limited spaces** (11 brewery spaces on the board per `[ET-B]`). Aim rail Links at them; without a developed network you will lack both beer and connections. Lines to watch: Shrewsbury–Walsall and the lines from Birmingham to Stafford and Burton-on-Trent. `[ET-I]`
- Place a Brewery when you can use a barrel yourself; a Brewery whose beer goes to opponents is worth less. `[ET-M]` `[ET-B]`
- **Where to build:** go first where there is competition, and count the Location cards per town (the sources read them off the game: e.g. Redditch, Walsall and Nuneaton have 1 card per era, Coalbrookdale 3, Uttoxeter 1 with 3 players and 2 with 4): a town with few cards is hard for others to build in without a network. `[SE]`
- **Developing away the level 1 Breweries?** The sources disagree: `[SE]` recommends developing them early and building Breweries early (brewery spaces are few and better used for higher levels); `[ET-B]` calls this a solid but overrated move:
  - *For:* a level 2 Brewery flipped in the Canal Era scores in both eras (10 VPs in total per the source).
  - *Against* `[ET-B]`*:* it costs a Develop action that cotton or manufactured goods need more; each player has only 7 Brewery tiles (component list, p. 1), so less beer over the game; a level 2+ Brewery placed early keeps a brewery space occupied in the Rail Era, usually inside your own network. The rulebook also warns about developing Breweries (p. 12).
  - *Alternative:* build the level 1 Brewery and later **overbuild your own Brewery** (p. 10): you lose the first tile's VPs but got its barrel and income. Overbuilding your own Breweries also creates beer late in the game when the board has run dry. `[ET-B]`

### Coal Mines
- **Highest income, lowest VPs.** Cheap, often paid back at once, good to get through the early game. But coal is a support industry, and every mine is one scoring action less. Building too many is the classic beginner mistake. `[ET-I]` `[ET-M]` `[Steam]`
- **Do build the first mine:** cheap, counts double for Links per the source, and you avoid developing it away later (see p. 12). Little coal is used in the Canal Era. `[ET-I]`
- **Your coal helps others:** they place industries or rail Links with it, which score more than your mine. In the Rail Era the income from a flipped mine is nearly irrelevant, while cheap coal is exactly what rail builders need. `[ET-M]`
- **Good moments:** the Coal Market is empty or expensive (cash at once, p. 9); you need the coal for your own next action; several players need coal this round. Best: build the mine, collect the Market money, and use the coal yourself with your second action, ideally for a double rail Link. `[Steam]` `[ET-I]` `[TTP]`
- **Bad moment:** coal is cheap in the Market and others already supply it. `[TTP]`
- **Location warnings:** don't build your first mine in Redditch (per the source no canal to Birmingham, Merchants on both sides, so nobody uses the coal, and you can't add an Iron Works there in the Canal Era because of the 1-tile limit, p. 10). `[ET-M]` Avoid Coal Mine spots that invite an opponent's Iron Works next to them, such as Coalbrookdale. `[Steam]`

### Iron Works
- **The opportunistic industry:** very good values, but only if it flips fast. Played well, an Iron Works pays for itself and for an early loan; played badly, it helps the opponents. `[ET-I]`
- Demand for iron is limited, and each player has only 4 Iron Works (p. 1): building one also **denies** that efficiency to others, which is why Iron Works are strongly linked to winning. Reason not to develop the level 1 Iron Works away: you want to play all four. `[ET-I]`
- **Support only:** there aren't enough to base a strategy on. Keep fitting cards in hand (the source names Dudley, Coventry, Coalbrookdale) and jump in when the chance comes. `[ET-I]`
- **Start of the game:** one Develop action takes enough iron to open Market spaces, so the next Iron Works flips at once (p. 9). Don't make this too easy for an opponent: Develop first, then place the Iron Works yourself. `[ET-I]`
- **"Iron battery" opening:** Iron Works, then 2–3 Develop actions with your own iron, then a level 2 selling tile near beer. `[TTP]` Iron can be built anywhere, since iron needs no connection (p. 8). `[HL]`
- **Level 1 Iron Works for income:** often worth building early; consider **overbuilding your own** level 1 Iron Works later in the Canal Era (p. 10). If you built a Link to bring coal to it, the overbuild uses the same Link. `[SE]`
- **Overbuilding opponents:** when no iron is left on tiles or in the Market, you may replace an opponent's Iron Works (same for coal, p. 10): a serious hole in their score. Remind everyone of this rule before the game. `[ET-I]`

### Cotton Mills
- **Expensive, but many VPs per card.** When good Links are gone and everyone has Breweries, flipping selling tiles is how you get ahead. `[ET-I]`
- **A game-long plan with early Develop actions.** Ideally you build none of the three level 1 tiles. Level 2 counts double for Links but brings few VPs; the goal is **level 3** (9 VPs each per the source, and several can be sold with one Sell action, p. 10). `[ET-I]`
- **Two ways in:** `[ET-I]`
  1. *Rush level 3 in the Canal Era:* three flipped level 3 mills score 54 VPs over both eras (the rulebook's own example: 18 VPs per mill, p. 12). Needs a lot of capital; you can fall behind and trail all game.
  2. *Smaller tiles:* clearly fewer points, but an even start into the Rail Era (own beer, loans paid off by coal, fewer Links needed).
- Either way, the main gain is having **good tiles to place in the Rail Era**, when brewery and iron spaces run out.
- Remove the level 1 mills early with Develop, ideally flipping your own Iron Works with the iron. `[Steam]` Cotton is rated best with **2 players** (room for many Develop actions); Kidderminster and Worcester are named as key cotton locations there. `[SE]` `[Steam]`
- **Cotton plan in detail** (2 players) `[SE]`:
  - Four Develop actions (2 tiles each, p. 11): (1) both level 1 Breweries; (2) two level 1 Cotton Mills; (3) the last level 1 and one level 2 Cotton Mill; (4) the other level 2 Cotton Mill plus, by situation, a level 1 Coal Mine, a level 1 Iron Works or a level 3 Cotton Mill.
  - In the Canal Era build **at least two level 3 Cotton Mills** and **two level 2 Breweries**, and sell the cotton with your own beer.
  - Then actions remain at the end of the Rail Era to build more cotton for many VPs.

### Manufacturers
- **The value changes with the level;** overall weaker than cotton (as much beer, fewer VPs and income), but competition, the map or your cards can make them the better path. Rated better with 3–4 players. `[ET-I]` `[SE]`
- Level by level, as the sources rate them (check the tiles):
  - **Levels 1–2:** good. Level 1 gives good income and counts double for Links (strong in Birmingham or Stoke-on-Trent); level 2 gives no income but stays for both eras and needs no coal. Solid actions that also use up your beer. `[ET-I]`
  - **Level 3:** a blocker: no Link value, so place it inside a rival's network to deny Link points; otherwise weak and expensive. `[ET-I]`
  - **Level 4:** poor. `[ET-I]`
  - **Levels 5–8:** strong scorers; level 5 needs 2 beer. With a couple of Manufacturers already on the board, Develop the weak middle levels away. Pivoting into these late is easier than into undeveloped cotton. `[ET-I]`
- **Manufactured goods plan in detail** (3–4 players, where actions are few and every built tile must be sure to sell) `[SE]`:
  - Only two Develop actions: (1) both level 1 Breweries; (2) one level 1 Manufacturer plus a level 1 Coal Mine or Iron Works.
  - In the Canal Era build **two level 2 Manufacturers** and **two level 2 Breweries**; sell with your own beer, Merchant beer only if yours was taken. Level 2 Manufacturers need no coal.
  - With 2 players: spend one more Develop action on levels 3 and 4, then surround Birmingham with Links and build there.
- Uses by icon `[Steam]`: double Link value (levels 1 and 5) next to **your** Links; no Link value (levels 3 and 7) next to **opponents'** Links; no beer needed (levels 3 and 8) for a late sale when beer is gone.

### Potteries
- **High risk, high reward:** the highest point ceiling, but easy to block and it leaves little room for anything else. Focus on pottery only if your starting hand and the Merchants in play support it. `[ET-I]` Generally not recommended; go for it only if the Merchants **and** your cards fit pottery and you lack the cards for cotton (2 players) or manufactured goods (3–4 players). `[SE]`
- **Develop for pottery:** both level 1 Breweries, possibly the level 1 Pottery. Levels 2 and 4 (which can't be developed, p. 11) are built mainly to reserve a space; building can be cheaper than developing. `[SE]`
- **Blocking:** a level 1 Pottery in the Rail Era scores and can take the space from someone heading for a level 5 Pottery. `[SE]`
- **Level 1** may be built in both eras (p. 12) and needs no Develop: a good opportunistic tile (10 VPs and 5 income levels per the source). Play it alongside other industries and flip it in the same Sell action. In the Canal Era it scores only once, like in the Rail Era, so build it early mainly to reach level 3. `[ET-I]` `[Steam]`
- **Level 1 + level 3 in the Canal Era** is the strong result, but a straight rush is unreliable: only four pottery locations, far apart; level 3 needs 2 beer; opponents block the spaces and take your beer. Plan two beers of your own, at most one from a Merchant. `[ET-I]` `[SE]`
- **Flexible line:** place level 1 early, keep level 3 in mind, count the actions needed; building in Coventry or Stoke-on-Trent doesn't commit you. Stafford and Worcester are named as key pottery locations. `[ET-I]` `[Steam]`
- **Rail Era:** with 2 barrels per Brewery (p. 9), levels 3 and 5 are a strong alternative to cotton, mostly for rail-heavy plans that already have Breweries and Links. `[ET-I]`
- Never build a Pottery you can't surely sell; if beer or route are not certain in the last rounds, switch to rail Links or a simpler sale. `[TTP]`

## 6. Canal Era

- **Purpose:** position and preparation, not points: industries in key locations for a good start into the Rail Era, Develop actions done, beer secured, one or two fast flips. `[Steam]` `[TTP]`
- **Develop early and often,** the most underrated action. Two reasons: level 2+ tiles built now score twice and count for Links twice (p. 7, 12); and higher levels give more per action. The price is the iron you leave for others; still, not developing is what you regret later. `[ET-M]`
- **Few canals.** The canal network is removed at era end (p. 7), there are few Link points, and 1 Link costs a whole action (p. 11). Build a canal for points only if it scores at least **4–5 VPs**. Useful canals reach an iron spot or coal, a Merchant, or a brewery line that becomes a rail route later. `[Steam]` `[SE]` `[TTP]`
- **Level 1 tiles** leave the board at era end (p. 7): fine if they flip quickly (income, beer, iron, an early Merchant bonus), poor if they wait. Don't place level 1 selling tiles "just to place". `[TTP]`
- **Don't open easy Iron Works for opponents:** let them build the canal to coal themselves. `[Steam]`
- **Last 1–2 actions of the era:** consider a final Develop so every Rail Era build is a good tile. `[TTP]`
- **Opening actions** `[SE]`:
  - *Loan:* as first player, take a Loan; you stay first and can build an Iron Works next round. Also as a later player if the players before you spent money.
  - *Develop:* the level 1 Breweries, so every later brewery card builds level 2 or higher. Develop with iron in mind only "maybe": not in a 2-player game if the player before you took a Loan, as it sets them up to build the Iron Works.
  - *Canal Link:* in a 2-player game only if the first player developed (they then spent more than your £3, so you move first). Then Loan and Iron Works: the iron goes to the Market and you are cash-positive at once. Don't connect to a Merchant with this Link; look for a future connection to coal.
- **Three opening templates** (adapt to your hand) `[TTP]`:

| Template | Sequence |
|---|---|
| Iron first | Iron Works → Develop 2–3 times → level 2 Cotton Mill/Manufacturer near beer → Sell |
| Beer anchor | Brewery within reach of two Merchants → Cotton Mill/Manufacturer → sell twice, your own beer first |
| Flexible | Develop, Iron Works, a Coal Mine only if the Market is expensive → secure a hub → enter the Rail Era with cash and beer |

## 7. Rail Era

- **Rail Links are the big scorer:** a rail Link is rarely worth less than 4 VPs and often up to 8; canals score far less. `[ET-M]`
- **Double rail Link** (2 Links for £15, 2 coal, 1 beer, p. 11): one of the best plays in the game, worth a worse turn order or a loan. Take beer from the board or place your own, build two Links, and repeat next turn if you can; keep going until the cost no longer pays. Plan it before the era starts. `[ET-M]`
- **First round:** coal for the first Links is the bottleneck (every rail Link needs a coal connection, p. 11). Build a Coal Mine if needed and use turn order to use that coal yourself. Entering the era without cash and beer for an immediate double rail is a common mistake. `[ET-I]` `[TTP]`
- **Where:** named strong spots are Uttoxeter, Worcester and Birmingham `[ET-M]`; Birmingham is the main hub, and Coventry, Worcester and Wolverhampton often decide the Link points `[TTP]`; lines to the Farm Breweries (Cannock, Kidderminster–Worcester) `[SE]`. Prefer a connected main line through hubs, beer and Merchants over scattered dead ends. `[TTP]`
- **Links before industries:** an industry can take Develop, Loan, Build and Sell actions, a Link only one; so first build as many valuable Links as possible, and when the best are gone, build your highest-value industries. `[SE]`
- **The good Links run out:** after that, industries decide the game, so you need selling tiles that are worth placing (developed cotton, high Manufacturers, Potteries). `[ET-M]` `[ET-I]`
- **Place only tiles you can flip:** route to the Merchant and beer first. `[TTP]`
- **"Empty, then sprint":** let opponents use up your coal or iron, then use the income and an early turn for a double rail Link plus a sale. `[TTP]`
- **Last 3–4 actions:** count the remaining beer and the Merchant spaces twice; look for the line that scores for you and takes a sale away from a rival. If your tiles are flipped, two well-placed rail Links can beat a mediocre sale. Overbuild your own low tiles with high ones if that scores more (p. 10). `[TTP]`

## 9. Cards and Scout

- Plan around the cards you hold: Location cards for your key towns are worth keeping (p. 9). If your hand blocks a build, a Develop, Sell or Loan is better than forcing a bad build. `[TTP]` `[ET-I]`
- **Scout sparingly:** it costs an action and 3 cards (p. 11). Worth it when the Wild cards unlock several later actions, for example the Brewery space you need and the sale after it, or for turn order (section 3). A "bad" move like a Scout can still be right in the long run. `[TTP]` `[ET-B]`

## 10. Common mistakes (summary)

1. Too many Coal Mines; coal that only helps opponents. `[ET-M]` `[Steam]`
2. Too little beer; not contesting Breweries. `[ET-M]` `[Steam]`
3. Not developing; building level 1 tiles that never matter. `[ET-M]` `[Steam]` `[TTP]`
4. Too many canals, too few rail Links; no double rail at the start of the Rail Era. `[Steam]` `[ET-M]` `[TTP]`
5. No focus: a bit of every industry, or two selling industries at once. `[Steam]` `[SE]`
6. Ignoring turn order and the "free" actions. `[Steam]`
7. Building coal or iron "because it flips" without gaining time, money or VPs from it. `[TTP]`
8. Selling tiles built without a sure Merchant route and beer (above all Potteries). `[TTP]`
9. Loans without a plan, or no loans at all. `[TTP]` (p. 12)
